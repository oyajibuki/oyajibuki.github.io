      This year's focus

      Each year you decide what to do with your time outside work: rest, have fun, study for a license, reskill, take on a side gig, or look for a new job. Overtime eats into all of it.

      Moments of decision

      Promotions, exams, job offers, going solo, marriage, a new baby, caring for a parent, relocation, burnout, and how to work after 65. Your choices change your life, but escaping the statistics of your first job is not easy.

      What the data is based on

      Real data: job counts, pay percentiles and education by occupation (BLS OEWS and Employment Projections), and women's share by occupation (CPS). Company-size pay gaps, promotion odds, license pass rates, burnout and business survival are game estimates informed by published research. Details in the research notes (in Japanese).

        Job encyclopedia

        The full Standard Occupational Classification (SOC 2018): 22 major groups, 94 minor groups and 830 detailed occupations. Highlighted jobs are ones you have held. The code to the right of each minor group is its ISCO-08 equivalent.

        Job counts from BLS Employment Projections (2025, including self-employment; a person with two jobs counts twice). Pay from OEWS May 2025: because OEWS has no breakdown by age, "prime-age pay" is the median × 1.15 (the CPS ratio of median weekly earnings at ages 45–54 to all ages). Women's share from CPS 2025, matched by occupation title (otherwise the minor-group average) and scaled so the total matches the CPS figure of 47.1%. Overtime and AI exposure are game estimates by ISCO group. Tags under an occupation are common job titles; their pay multipliers are game estimates.
